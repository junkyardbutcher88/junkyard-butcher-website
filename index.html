The Process

Most brands go looking for something new. I go digging for what's already there — and make it work harder.

Every project starts the same way, before a single new asset gets made: an audit. I go through what you've already built — old posts, an abandoned list, a logo nobody's touched in years — and find the parts with real cutting edge. That's the raw material. Everything else gets carved away.

From there it's the two frameworks below, applied to whatever we're building — a content calendar, a lifecycle sequence, a full identity system — so the work compounds instead of starting from zero on every single project.

How We Work

Two Frameworks.
No Guesswork.

Systems thinking isn't the opposite of gritty creative work — it's what makes it repeatable instead of a lucky accident.

01 / TRUST

The 7-11-4

A buyer needs roughly seven hours of content, across eleven touchpoints, on four different platforms before they trust you enough to hand over money. It's a rule of thumb, not a law of physics — but it's a useful one. We don't leave that stacking up to chance — we build the content and lifecycle system that puts those touches in front of the right person on purpose.

02 / FOCUS

The 80/20 Hybrid

Twenty percent of what a brand already does drives eighty percent of the results. Before we create anything new, we dig through everything you've already built — every post, every list, every scrap of brand equity — find the twenty percent that's actually working, and cut the rest loose.

Farm Fiend x Rich Kids on LSD

Real show, real venue, real video — this one isn't anonymized. Farm Fiend was sponsoring Rich Kids on LSD's Southern California tour and wanted the LA stop — Telegram Ballroom, March 2025 — captured the way the scene actually feels: handheld, gritty, unmistakably punk, not a polished corporate recap. Drove down and shot the entire night on an iPhone 15 Pro Max — no crew, no gear bag. Same rule behind every Junkyard Butcher project: keep the kit minimal, let the vision and the constraints drive the work, and bring in more gear only when a job actually calls for it.
